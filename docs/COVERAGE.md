# Active coverage, regression evidence and procedural limits

`factory/constitution_coverage.yaml` covers C70–C93. It requires either an exactly resolved active callable with named regression references or a specific limitation rationale. C70 (authorization of policy changes) and C78 (generalization of pattern rules) are procedural obligations. The remaining 22 declarations name local mechanisms. That count measures references, not scientific completeness or resistance to arbitrary source mutations.

`verify-constitution-coverage` checks IDs, declaration shape, callable reachability and regression existence. Both certification paths execute registered attacks before research readiness; their per-entry scopes distinguish validation, freeze, run/audit and archive checks. These are concrete observed regressions rather than a claim that every attack traversed the whole lifecycle. The focused mutation campaign and matched seeded-fault benchmark provide evidence about their selected checks; they do not establish complete mutation coverage.

| Constitution rule | Implemented support | Remaining procedural judgment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. No fabricated evidence | Source/cohort/ID joins, declared provenance hashes, receipt/output checks, heuristic producer scans | Truthful acquisition and honest source use; same-user fabricated inputs cannot be ruled out |
| 2. Freeze decisions | Frozen plan and byte bindings; epoch/amendment records | Independently witnessed preregistration and undisclosed external experiments |
| 3. Separate exploration/confirmation | Disclosed holdout consumption; reused holdouts block confirmatory adaptation | Honest disclosure of test access outside the local workspace |
| 4. Verify transformations | Independently recomputed metrics, IDs, split and denominator joins | Domain operator semantics and source authenticity |
| 5. Learn before comparing | Shared contiguous trace, stopping/checkpoint/budget checks | Optimization adequacy, learning-rate choice and model suitability |
| 6. Respect independence | Group-paired inference; seed-only fixed-corpus scope; multiplicity/precision/feasibility | Correct independence hierarchy, representative sampling and interval adequacy |
| 7. Compare fairly | Planned baseline identities and supporting evidence | Credible/current alternatives and matched tuning opportunity |
| 8. Test mechanisms | Executed ablation cells/replication; sensitivity levels; alias disclosure | Operationally isolated interventions and causal interpretation |
| 9. Measure hardware | Trial/warmup/sustained interval and telemetry row checks | Honest instrumentation, synchronization and sensor calibration |
| 10. Review cold | Current artifact hashes, nine specific topics, objections, mode disclosure | Reviewer independence, correctness and context contamination |
| 11. Report boundary | Executed checks, diagnostics, status/assurance and human digest | Human inspection of the material evidence and scientific acceptance |
| 12. Keep workflow simple | Agent-operated CLI, single plan, digest and authorized handoff | Consequential human decisions and authorized resources |

The supplied F-001–F-024 forensic findings informed the v3 rules, but this is a limited incident-derived basis. Parser/metric tests and clean/adversarial fixtures improve mechanical confidence. `run_seeded_fault_benchmark.py` reports per-family detection and clean-control rejection for 36 synthetic projects with matched controls, including observed unsupported faults and conditional binomial intervals. These are constructed cases, not a representative real-project study, per-rule rates for all rules, or a universal detection guarantee. Qualitative false-positive analyses for D-074–D-102 appear in `factory/dynamic_rules.md`.

Historical v2.6 numbering remains in inactive sources. Active related-rule links use Rules 1–12 and C70–C93 rather than implying missing historical principles are current policy.

C93 binds the project publication boundary to real Git index/history inspection, chained hooks and concrete commit/push regressions. Public file allowlists and exact binary approvals remain private. Local guardrails cannot inspect external PR/issue content or prevent their owner from bypassing hooks.
